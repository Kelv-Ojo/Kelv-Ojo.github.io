The retention question

Which groups in the IBM Telco sample have higher churn, and what should a business investigate before choosing an intervention? I compared contract and tenure segments in SQL and Python.

Data & preparation

The educational snapshot contains 7,043 customers. Customer IDs are unique; 1,869 customers are labelled churned. Eleven blank TotalCharges values belong to customers with zero tenure. Those values remain missing and those customers remain in the churn denominators.

Method & uncertainty

I compared groups using SQL aggregation, CTEs and window functions, then reconciled counts with pandas. Tenure bands are 0–12, 13–24, 25–48 and 49+ months. Segment ranking requires at least 50 customers. Contract charts include descriptive 95% Wilson intervals.

A sensitivity analysis applies the same pooled tenure-band distribution to every contract type. It checks how much the headline comparison depends on tenure mix; it does not remove every confounder.

The month-to-month, 0–12 month group has 51.4% churn across 1,994 customers. The overall contract gap falls from 39.88 to 35.59 percentage points after pooled-tenure standardisation. The association remains, but its size depends on customer mix.

Proposed business action

Investigate onboarding and service friction for newer month-to-month customers. Then test a randomised retention pilot with a defined measurement window and a control group. Measure incremental retention and contribution margin after offer costs.

No saving or retention improvement has been realised by this analysis. Extending contracts is not shown to cause retention.

Limitations & lessons

These are cross-sectional sample proportions, not monthly churn hazards. The dataset has no timestamps, acquisition cohorts, margins or intervention outcomes. Intervals do not establish population representativeness. Tenure adjustment is a useful sensitivity check, not a causal model.
