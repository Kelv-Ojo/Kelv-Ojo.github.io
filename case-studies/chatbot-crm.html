Enquiry Desk

Turning a chatbot conversation into a customer record, a follow-up task and a useful service report.

Personal portfolio prototype built with coding assistance. All customers and workload figures below are fictional.

The problem

A suggested chatbot answer alone leaves no reliable record of who asked, what needs attention or whether someone followed up. This project connects those steps in a small, inspectable Python application.

Explore the workflow

This is a click-through explanation using the project's fixture data, not a hosted chatbot or live CRM connection.

A customer asks a question

Alex Demo: “Do you offer international shipping?”

The application finds or creates the customer by email, records the enquiry and stores a suggested reply. New enquiries start with status New.

The default engine matches normalised phrases exactly. It is not AI inference.

A person owns the next step

Unclear questions are flagged for review. An advisor can move the record between New, In progress and Resolved.

In the fixture, Alex's first enquiry is marked Resolved by a scripted status update. A reply suggestion does not close a record automatically.

Reconcile the workload

8 enquiries · 4 customers · 7 open · 4 flagged for review

The 50% review flag rate uses all eight captured enquiries as its denominator. These counts describe the fictional fixture, not model accuracy or customer outcomes.

What I built

SQLite contacts and enquiries, duplicate-email handling, repeat-request protection, human follow-up statuses and reporting in Streamlit. Tests cover the CRM logic and an application smoke test.

Integration and limitations

The optional HubSpot adapter finds or creates a fictional contact only. It has been tested with mocks, not a live account. It does not synchronise enquiry notes or tickets. The original neural model remains optional and its current runtime has not been verified.

No commercial deployment, time savings or model-performance results are claimed.
