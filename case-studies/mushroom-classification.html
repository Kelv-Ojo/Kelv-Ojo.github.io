Looking beyond classification accuracy

A revised analysis developed from my 7072CEM machine learning coursework, using the UCI Mushroom educational benchmark. The portfolio revision was prepared with AI assistance.

The question

How do three classifiers compare on categorical data, and what can overall accuracy hide about missed positive cases?

Data and method

The data contains 8,124 hypothetical samples and 22 categorical features. Of these, 2,480 stalk-root values are question marks. The revised analysis learns imputation and encoding within each training fold. Logistic regression also uses scaling and PCA inside the pipeline. A decision tree and categorical naive Bayes provide comparisons, alongside a majority-class baseline.

A stratified split reserves 1,625 test rows, including 783 poisonous labels. Five-fold cross-validation uses only the remaining 6,499 training rows. All pre-specified models are reported.

The naive Bayes model misses 96 poisonous-labelled test records despite overall accuracy above 93%. This shows why the denominator and type of error matter alongside a headline score.

What I improved

The original report contained inconsistent metric descriptions, and the notebook fitted some preprocessing before cross-validation. The revised code puts learned transformations inside pipelines, adds a baseline and exports exact confusion matrices. Its results supersede conflicting numbers in the earlier report.

Limits

This is an educational benchmark, not a tool for identifying edible mushrooms. A perfect score on one row-level split does not establish safety or generalisation to new species, regions or field observations. Species groups are unavailable, and hypothetical examples may share patterns across the split.

Recommendation and success measures

Proposed action — Check for leakage and evaluate errors on a separate benchmark split.

How I’d measure success — Report missed positives alongside accuracy and baseline performance. This educational model is not suitable for mushroom safety decisions.
